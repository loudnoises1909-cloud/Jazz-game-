# Bat Cave

A small collection of toddler games. The start screen is a menu; in a game,
press and hold the 🏠 button (top left) for a second to come back to it.

## Busy Board

A wooden busy board with six gadgets and no rules:

- Big button: a T-Rex jack-in-the-box.
- Light switch: the bulb glows in changing colours and the sleeping bat flies
  off across the whole screen; switch it off and the bat comes back to sleep.
- Music box crank: gears turn and the game's tune plays note by note.
- Peekaboo door: a T-Rex pops out, wearing a different hat or bow tie each time.
- Rocket lever: countdown, then the rocket leaves its box and flies up across the
  screen into a starry sky before landing back on its pad.
- Prize wheel: flick or tap to spin; whatever stops at the top (candy, lollipop,
  toy car, ball, T-Rex toy or star) pops out as the prize, and dozens of them rain
  down from the top of the screen.

Trying all six gets a small celebration.

## Bat Bath

A messy bat sits in a clawfoot tub: scrub with the sponge (the mess fades and
bubbles pile up), rinse with the shower, dry with the towel and fluff with the
hair dryer. Three bats per game: muddy, berry juice, then paint splats. The
rubber duck squeaks whenever you like.

## Bat Band

Five bats on a stage, each singing its own note when tapped, plus a xylophone
(tap or slide), a drum, maracas and a record player that starts the music and
makes the bats dance. No goals.

## Froggy Tag

A friendly maze chase. The maze walls are nature obstacles (streams, rows of
trees, rock piles and fallen logs), each with its own bump sound.

In short: Froggy wants to play tag. Drag anywhere on the maze and
the bat follows your finger along the paths, one square at a time; walls block
it (a little "boing"), a breadcrumb trail shows where it has been and every step
plays the next note of a tune. Five mazes grow from 3×3 to 6×4 (turned upright
when the tablet is), and from the second maze Froggy hops away once when the bat
gets close. If nothing happens for a few seconds, glowing stars show the next
steps. Catching Froggy fills a lily pad at the top.

## Bat Doctor

A toddler game. Five pink bats (Doowy, Hoowy, Tooby, Boopy, Zoomy) each need the
doctor. Every visit follows the same routine:

1. Listen to the heart with the stethoscope.
2. Check the temperature with the thermometer.
3. Fix the problem with the right tool:
   - scrape → the bandage with the same shape and colour
   - bump on the head → ice bag
   - splinter → tweezers
   - hungry and weak (cold, tired heart) → shoe box, blanket, bottle of milk
4. Tap the bat 3 times to fill the stars, and it flies to its picture frame.

Heal all 5 to start the party. Drag a tool, or just tap it.

After healing, the bat flies home under the moon and a family postcard collects
in its frame. Holiday postcards (Halloween, Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year,
Easter, 4th of July) are used when that holiday is close; otherwise everyday ones
(beach, camping, picnic, snow day, birthday, dinosaur museum, stargazing, rainy
day) are shuffled.

- The game is `index.html` (no build step). Made for tablets and phones (touch),
  works with a mouse too.
- Grown-ups: press and hold the ⚙︎ button (top right) for ~2 seconds for settings
  (sound effects, device voice, postcard theme).

## The voice

The game ships with all lines spoken by **Kokoro** (open-source neural text-to-speech;
the model is Apache 2.0, kokoro-onnx is MIT), voice `af_heart`, generated by:

    pip install kokoro-onnx soundfile        # plus ffmpeg
    python3 tools/generate-voice.py --model kokoro-v1.0.onnx --voices voices-v1.0.bin

(model files: https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx/releases/tag/model-files-v1.0).
Options: `--voice af_bella` or a blend like `--voice "af_heart:0.7,af_bella:0.3"`,
`--warmth 1.0` to skip the slight pitch lift, `--only welcome,healed` to redo a few
lines, `--samples DIR` to write a comparison of several voices. The pace of each line
follows its moods. Lines recorded in the studio are never overwritten.

## Recording the voice

The voice ships with the app; players never record anything.

1. Open `studio.html` from an `https://` address (or in Chrome on a computer) and
   record each of the 25 lines. Takes are trimmed and levelled automatically and
   kept in that browser until you download them.
   Click **Try in game** at any point to play the real game with the takes
   recorded so far (preview mode; nothing is uploaded).
2. Click **Download all (zip)**, unzip, and put the `.wav` files in `voice-takes/`.
3. Run `bash tools/build-voices.sh` (needs ffmpeg). It writes `voice/<line>.mp3`
   and `voice/voices.json`.

Any line without a recording falls back to the device's built-in voice. Each line
is written as short phrases (separated by `|`) with a mood per phrase (happy,
excited, celebrate, question, gentle, cozy, worried, slow), so the built-in voice
changes pace and pitch phrase by phrase. The game prefers the most natural voice
installed (Premium, Enhanced or Natural voices first), and a grown-up can choose
another in settings. The
line list in `studio.html` must match `LINES` in `index.html`.

## Version 2 (3D): `v2/index.html`

All five games in real-time 3D, in a moonlit cave. Open it over http (e.g. `python3 -m http.server`), like the main game.

- **Bat Doctor**: listen to the heart with the stethoscope, match the bandage to the scrape's shape, tap to heal; the bat flies out of the cave to the moon.
- **Froggy Tag**: a grassy maze diorama on the table with hedges, rocks, logs, streams and trees. Hold a finger down and the bat steps towards it; catch the frog on its lily pad. Five mazes, each bigger; from the second, Froggy hops away when the bat gets close.
- **Busy Board**: a T-Rex jack-in-the-box, a lamp that wakes a sleepy bat, gears with a music-box tune, a door with a T-Rex behind it, a rocket that flies out of the cave into fireworks, and a prize wheel whose prize rains onto the table. Trying all six sends a flock of bats across the cave.
- **Bat Bath**: a clawfoot tub. The dirt (mud, berry, paint) is painted into the bat's fur; the sponge cleans where it rubs, the shower pops the bubbles, the towel dries the wet fur, the hair dryer fluffs it up. Three bats.
- **Bat Band**: five bats on a curtained stage sing their own note when tapped; a xylophone, drum and maracas to play; the record player starts a dance with sweeping spotlights.

How it is built:
- [three.js](https://threejs.org) r160 (MIT, see `v2/THREE-LICENSE.txt`) and its bloom post-processing add-ons, bundled in `v2/` so it works offline. No other dependencies.
- `v2/engine.js` holds what the games share: renderer, the cave set, the furry bat (real-time fur, textured eyes with eyelids, veined wings), sound effects, the voice lines in `voice/`, particles, tweens and input. `v2/main.js` is the menu; each game is one file in `v2/games/`.
- The cave is lit like a film set: a flickering lantern, moonlight and a light beam through a ragged opening, soft glow on light sources, fog and a vignette. On devices that cannot keep up it turns the glow off and lowers the resolution by itself.
- Tap the house button to go back to the menu of all five games (the running game is stopped cleanly, no page reload). The menu links to the classic 2D game.
